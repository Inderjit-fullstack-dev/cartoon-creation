# Story — What If an Asteroid Hit the Moon?

**Hook:** The Moon is hit all the time. What would it take for us to notice from Earth?

## Beats
1. **Hook:** A giant asteroid streaks toward the Moon.
2. **Setup:** The Moon has no atmosphere to burn it up, so it arrives at full speed (typically tens of thousands of km/h).
3. **Central question:** What happens, and does it reach us?
4. **Impact:** A 1 km asteroid carves a crater more than 10 km wide in seconds; the rock vaporizes and melts.
5. **Silence:** No air, so no sound and no mushroom cloud. Debris flies out in perfect arcs.
6. **Seen from Earth:** A bright flash. This is real: in March 2013, a roughly 40 kg meteoroid made a flash visible to telescopes.
7. **Consequence:** Some ejecta reaches Earth. Lunar meteorites are already found on our planet.
8. **Orbit and tides:** A 1 km asteroid is tiny next to the Moon (billions of times less massive), so the Moon's orbit and Earth's tides stay the same.
9. **Climax:** The scale: the Moon's south pole basin is ~2,500 km wide from an ancient impact; the Moon itself likely formed from a giant impact.
10. **Ending:** The Moon wears its scars. Next time you see it, you're looking at a record of impacts. CTA.

## Accuracy notes
- Facts: no lunar atmosphere; constant small impacts; 2013 flash (NASA); lunar meteorites found on Earth; South Pole–Aitken basin ~2,500 km; giant-impact origin is the leading theory.
- Assumptions (flagged as "imagine"): the 1 km asteroid and its speed (~20 km/s typical order of magnitude).
- Avoided: claims of Earth-threatening debris or tide changes.
