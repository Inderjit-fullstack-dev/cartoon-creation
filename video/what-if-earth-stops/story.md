# Story — "What if the Earth Stopped Rotating?"

**Format:** YouTube Shorts · 1080×1920 · 58.1 s (locked) · 6 scenes · 2D cartoon
**Tone:** Witty documentary, general audience, family-friendly
**Ending:** Wonder — no call to action

---

## Logline

You are riding a spinning planet at 1,670 km/h and you've never noticed — until the ride stops, and physics takes over for the remaining fifty-odd seconds.

## Story beats

1. **Strong opening hook — "You are moving."**
   Earth laps through space like a spinning fairground carousel. Every one of us is a passenger at up to 1,670 kilometers per hour at the equator — we just don't feel it, because everything moves *together*.

2. **Interesting setup — "The ride stalls."**
   The hypothetical: Earth stops rotating. Instantly. Not slowing over centuries — a hard stop, *this second*.

3. **Central hypothetical question** — spoken plainly in Scene 2 and visualized as everything detaching from the ground.

4. **Progressive development one — "Inertia doesn't attend the meeting."**
   Inertia is the great bureaucrat of physics: everything not bolted to the crust — you, your car, the air, the oceans — keeps moving eastward at the speed the ground used to be going. At the equator that's supersonic. Anything loose becomes a passenger thrown off a carousel.

5. **Progressive development two — "The atmosphere never got the memo."**
   The air keeps going and becomes a global sandblaster: permanent, planet-wide hurricane-force winds — the well-known modeled outcome of a suddenly stationary Earth under a still-moving sky.

6. **Conflict/suspense — "Water takes sides."**
   The equatorial bulge is a *spin* artifact. Take the spin away and it slowly relaxes: water migrates from the equator toward the poles over centuries — draining some latitudes, drowning others. Earth redraws its own map.

7. **Unexpected consequence / climax — "One day becomes one year."**
   Day and night only flip because the planet rotates. Without rotation, a single "day" becomes one full orbit around the Sun: six months of daylight, six months of night on each face.

8. **Memorable ending — "The longest sunset."**
   The Earth doesn't end in fire or ice — it becomes a planet of stripes: a scorched face, a frozen back, and a narrow twilight ring at the edge where the Sun never finishes setting. The video closes on a slow, gorgeous, endless sunset — wonder, not doom.

## Character design

**"Pip"** — a small, round astronaut-everyman doodle in a tangerine hoodie, cream skin, one hair curl, seen from behind or in profile. Pip is the audience's avatar: standing in a field (S1), tumbling (S2), clinging to a lamppost (S3), away in S4 (globe solo breather), a tiny cameo on the terminator (S5), and finally still on the cliff edge watching the endless sunset (S6).
One consistent character, one consistent identity: **tangerine hoodie + cream skin + single hair curl** — unchanged in every appearance.

## Fiction vs. physics ledger

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Equator surface speed ≈ 1,670 km/h | **Fact** (2πR / 24 h) |
| Speed scales by latitude (cos θ) | **Fact** — narration says "up to" 1,670 km/h |
| Instant stop → eastward flight of loose objects | **Physically sound assumption** for the hypothetical |
| Global wind scouring / planet-wide hurricanes | **Modeled outcome** — presented as the dominant predicted consequence |
| Oceans migrate poleward; bulge relaxes | **Fact (effect)** / **Assumption (timescale shown fast for art)** |
| Day = one year on a non-rotating (but orbiting) Earth | **Fact** — solar day becomes the orbital period |
| Twilight ring / scorched-frozen hemispheres | **Simplified but standard illustration** |

Only the equatorial speed is stated numerically in narration; visuals carry the rest.

## Deliberately avoided

- No fireball explosions — honest physics is already dramatic
- No docked CTA; last beat is calm wonder
- No on-screen captions (subtitles disabled per brief)
